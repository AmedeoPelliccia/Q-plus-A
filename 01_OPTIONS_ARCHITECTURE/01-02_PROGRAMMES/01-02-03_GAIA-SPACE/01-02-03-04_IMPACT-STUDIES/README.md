# GAIA-SPACE — Charter for Shared Horizons

**Issue 0.2 · PROPOSED — adoption follows the amendment procedure of the digital constitution (`00-04`); merge alone does not ratify a programme charter.** Expresses the direction set by Amedeo Pelliccia on 30 September 2026 (Italian source text: *Carta per orizzonti condivisi*, v0.1). Programme node: `01_OPTIONS_ARCHITECTURE/01-02_PROGRAMMES/01-02-03_GAIA-SPACE/`.

> **Explore the Universe to widen shared knowledge, and take responsibility for the consequences — on Earth and in the space environments we reach.**

## 1. Mission

GAIA-SPACE develops astronomical research, exploration and space technology to widen shared knowledge, improve living conditions, strengthen the scientific and technical capacity of communities, and contribute to ecological protection. Every project states its objectives, beneficiaries, resources, impacts and the evidence needed to judge its results.

Knowledge of the Universe has scientific and cultural value of its own. Economic and social applications are sought and documented, never promised before they are demonstrated.

Lunar, Martian and other planetary exploration belongs in the programme when its scientific question or collective purpose, its alternatives, the sharing of its results and its impacts can be assessed. **Colonial dominion, exclusive appropriation, and shifting social or ecological costs onto others are incompatible with this charter. Personal prestige, symbolic presence and the display of power are not sufficient justification for a mission.** The choice between crewed and robotic means is argued from objectives, risks and resources; neither is presumed preferable.

## 2. Place in Q+A

This charter directs a **programme**. It does not create taxonomy: technologies map to `100–199` S-STA nodes and other applicable bands, and a social purpose alone never mints a taxonomy code. It does not create a parallel record system: missions are described by the SBS views that already exist, federated over one identity system (`AMPEL360-SBS-ID-CM-002` applies to every programme's SBS by adoption).

## 3. Five dimensions — and where each one is recorded

Every project declares its primary purpose and assesses every relevant dimension. No project must maximise all indicators or earn a commercial return. An inapplicable dimension is justified; an unknown value stays declared unknown — **null is not zero**.

| Dimension | Question | Recorded in |
|---|---|---|
| **Scientific** | Which question, and what knowledge beyond the existing? | ReqBS (mission questions as requirements) · EBS (data, methods, reproducibility) — publication counts alone are insufficient |
| **Social** | Who can use the results, who decides, who bears the costs? | ReqBS stakeholder needs (beneficiaries, affected groups, access conditions) · RBS (costs and risks borne by others) |
| **Technological** | Which capability is new or better, against which alternative? | PBS · TPMS (performance against the alternative, declared maturity, reliability, interoperability, repairability) |
| **Ecological** | Which pressures across the whole life cycle? | TPMS life-cycle measures · RBS · S-STA `107` contamination and cleanliness, `108` debris, collision avoidance and re-entry, `178` end-of-life passivation and disposal · optical and radio interference with astronomy |
| **Long-term** | Can commitments be kept and the mission closed responsibly? | CBS (life-cycle cost, end-of-mission provision) · LBS (maintenance, replacement) · WBS (data and skills preservation) |

Numerical targets, thresholds and verification methods are set per mission and per authorised phase; this charter invents no universal technical limit. Where available models cannot characterise an impact, the uncertainty stays visible.

## 4. Programme commitments

**4.1 Purpose and proportionality.** Every proposal is compared with realistic options — reuse of data, existing infrastructure, ground instruments, shared payloads, a different architecture, or not intervening — and states its additional advantage and what the resources would otherwise have done. Complexity and distance are not measures of usefulness.

**4.2 Shared knowledge.** Plan publication, documentation, interoperable formats, attribution and reuse, with explicit licences for what can be released; every restriction has a stated reason and, where possible, a review date. The repository has no general licence today: this charter grants no rights over existing content and applies none retroactively — openness is enacted by authorised decisions on individual artefacts.

**4.3 Benefits and participation.** Identify users and communities, access conditions, borne costs and ways to take part; consult effectively on ground infrastructure and territorial impacts; keep objections and the changes they caused traceable. The quality of access counts as much as the number of users.

**4.4 Integrity of partners and funding.** Compatibility criteria refer to documented conduct and activities, the origin and conditions of capital, conflicts of interest and actual adherence to the mission — applied equally to internal management, partners and funders, proportionately to the relationship, with stated reasons, a right of reply and review. A generic moral label is not evidence. Enforcement through statutes and contracts is a separate act, bound by the legal regime of each existing relationship.

**4.5 Ecological and space responsibility.** Cover production, supply, ground infrastructure, launch, operations, data processing and end of mission; reusability is judged on the life cycle, never credited automatically. Missions concerned carry responsibilities and resources for debris management, passivation, collision avoidance, disposal, planetary protection and the mitigation of optical and radio interference. **Protecting astronomical observation is an explicit criterion: a mission can produce data and obstruct other observations at the same time.**

**4.6 Integrity of evidence.** Observed results, calculations, simulations, estimates and hypotheses are kept distinct; provenance and limits of data — synthetic and AI-generated included — are declared; models, parameters and versions are kept so conclusions can be rebuilt. A negative result or a falsified hypothesis is a useful scientific outcome, never retrofitted into a commercial promise.

**4.7 Continuity and reinvestment.** The economic plan states pay, skills, investment, justified reserves, dissemination and future liabilities; where services or licences earn revenue, remuneration, accessibility and reinvestment follow transparent criteria. No universal percentages are set here. **Monetised social value is never booked as money available to pay salaries or fund missions.**

## 5. Three ledgers, and the discipline of valuation

Assessment keeps three accounts apart: **scientific** (question, method, knowledge, reusable data and capability) · **economic and social** (incremental benefits and costs against an explicit alternative, who is involved, when, with what uncertainty) · **financial** (actual income and expenditure, funding, commitments — the CBS).

For the monetisable part, the social net present value is

$$NPV_{social}=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Delta B_t-\Delta C_t]}{(1+r)^t}$$

with ΔB and ΔC incremental against the declared alternative, *r* a justified discount rate, *T* the horizon; territory, population, currency, price year and risk treatment stated. Present adoption and failure scenarios and sensitivity; never count the same benefit twice through turnover, wages, taxes, user savings or transfers inside the perimeter; record harms and end-of-life costs.

**A valuation is an analysis record and follows CM-001 C1.6.** Counterfactual, discount rate, horizon, scenarios and attribution are **declared and content-bound before the benefits are computed** — the same pre-solver discipline applied to structural analysis, for the same reason: criteria chosen after the result are not evidence. Ex-ante valuation informs a decision; ex-post valuation checks what happened.

Fundamental research may be selected on scientific and cultural merit with no estimable monetary NPV; the portfolio provides for it explicitly. **An unknown commercial return is neither zero benefit nor a demonstrated one.**

## 6. Decisions — bound to baselines, not to a parallel engine

Review outcomes are expressed through the integrated baseline of the mission (`AMPEL360-SBS-LC-CM-003`, adopted by reference once ratified):

| Outcome | Meaning | Baseline expression |
|---|---|---|
| **ALLOW** | mandatory conditions of the phase met, evidence adequate, authority authorises activities and resources within an explicit perimeter | `release_decision` taken on the exact set of revisions |
| **WARN** | non-blocking shortfalls with owner, corrective action and date — accompanies a decision, never authorises by itself | `open_items` with owner and due date |
| **ESCALATE** | material doubt, insufficient evidence or conflict; affected activities stay unauthorised, only already-authorised studies continue | `status: PROPOSED`, decision pending authority |
| **BLOCK** | proven incompatibility with the fundamental commitments, or a mandatory condition of the phase violated; reformulate or close | mandatory gate criterion `met: false` |

**No dimension compensates another:** a high score in one never offsets a serious violation in another, and no average may hide rights violations, manipulated evidence, or exceeded environmental and safety limits. Conditions are proportional to phase — authorising a feasibility study authorises neither construction nor launch. Every decision keeps authority, reasons, documents, version, conditions and review date; substantial change to mission, funding, beneficiaries or impacts reopens the relevant assessments (CM-003 §3).

## 7. Mission dossier — a derived view, not a new register

The dossier is **generated from the mission's SBS and baseline**, never maintained by hand beside them; every field below is a reference.

| Field | Source |
|---|---|
| Identity, configuration, phase, owner | PBS mission node · baseline record |
| Question and purpose · alternatives and counterfactual | ReqBS · impact study (§5) |
| Beneficiaries and affected groups | ReqBS stakeholder needs · RBS |
| Five dimensions — indicators, baselines, targets, thresholds, reasons for non-applicability | TPMS · ReqBS · EBS |
| Technology transfer — plausible applications, adopters, proof of adoption | impact study · EBS |
| Budgets and resources — full cost, cash, funding, reserves, future liabilities | CBS |
| Environment and end of mission | TPMS · RBS · S-STA `107` `108` `178` |
| Openness and rights | licence decisions per artefact |
| Evidence — provenance, type, method, version, limits | EBS |
| Governance — reviewers, conflicts, decisions, conditions, review date | baseline record · `LIFECYCLE-GATES` |

## 8. Candidate lines of work

Possibilities to assess, not existing, funded or approved missions: **astronomy and cosmology on shared data** (first evidence: the question, an inventory of available data, comparison with existing studies) · **Earth observation for climate and territory** (a need agreed with users and proof that the information improves a decision) · **space weather and risk surveillance** (performance against existing services and the link from information to mitigation) · **efficient water, energy and materials systems** (physical balance, life cycle, comparison with mature terrestrial alternatives) · **in-space robotics and servicing** (whole-system analysis including the servicing missions) · **lunar and planetary science** (scientific justification, choice of means, site protection, plan for access to results).

2. Link it from the constitution articles and from this programme's README; keep principles, operational requirements and technology taxonomy separate.

Seed the S-STA standards register (`100-700_Sources-Standards-and-Registers-Governance`) with the references the ecological dimension needs, editions fixed in the register: COSPAR Planetary Protection Policy · IADC Space Debris Mitigation Guidelines · ISO 24113 · ITU Radio Regulations · IAU guidance on dark and quiet skies.
4. Apply the dossier to one pilot proposal, reusing existing data and analyses before any new infrastructure.
5. Automate only after the criteria and the quality of evidence have been tested; automation must handle missing data, versions and authority without turning a forecast into an observed result.
