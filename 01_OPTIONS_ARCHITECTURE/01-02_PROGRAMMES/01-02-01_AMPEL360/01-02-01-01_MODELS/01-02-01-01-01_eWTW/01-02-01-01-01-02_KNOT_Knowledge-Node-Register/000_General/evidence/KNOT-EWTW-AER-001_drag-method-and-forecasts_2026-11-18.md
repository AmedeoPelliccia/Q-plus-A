## What follows for the eWTW

- **The polar of the screening has a public method.** Statement 1 is enough for a polar of first order: a zero-lift drag from the wetted area and a skin-friction coefficient, an induced drag from the aspect ratio and a span efficiency, and the wave drag apart (statement 2). The reviewer checked the constant by arithmetic: half the square root of π times 0.85 over 0.003 is 14.9. The method needs the wetted area and the aspect ratio of each option, which no record holds yet (`KNU-EWTW-AER-001-03`).
- **The claim of the report of 2026-11-17 can now be checked, and is not yet.** That report said that handbook values agree with a lift-to-drag ratio of 15 for the reference type. The check needs the wetted area of the Embraer 190, which is not read (finding 8). It is the first run of `KNU-EWTW-AER-001-02`.
- **A computed value for a type in service is on record.** 16.33 for an optimised 737-800 (statement 3) is the only cruise lift-to-drag ratio of a current airliner in these records. It is calculated, and for a larger aircraft with a higher aspect ratio than the reference type. It does not contradict the 15 of the screening.
- **The forecasts stay the missing source.** They are the documents that say what the technologies are expected to reach by 2027 and 2037. Three attempts have failed for a reason that is not in the sources: the files do not open from this workspace. The author can attach Doc 10127, the technology appendix of the report on the long-term goal, and issue 12 of the database of EASA.

These are consequences drawn by the reviewer. They are not quotations.
