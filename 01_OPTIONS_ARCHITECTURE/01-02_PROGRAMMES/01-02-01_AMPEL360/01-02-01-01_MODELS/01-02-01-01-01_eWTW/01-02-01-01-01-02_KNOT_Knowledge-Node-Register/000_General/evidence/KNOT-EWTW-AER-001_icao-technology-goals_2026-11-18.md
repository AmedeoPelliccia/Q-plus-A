## Read at the source

### The review of the independent experts, 2019

The source is *Aviation and the Environment: Outlook*, by Cumpsty, Mavris and Kirby, pages 24 to 38 of the ICAO Environmental Report 2019. The table of goals was read twice, in the copy of ICAO and in the copy of the United Nations Digital Library, with the same figures.

| # | Statement | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The experts took one reference aircraft of 2017 for each class: the Gulfstream G650ER for business jets, the Embraer E190-E2 for regional jets, the Airbus A320neo for single-aisle aircraft and the Airbus A350-900 for twin-aisle aircraft. Fuel burn data are a competitive matter, so the experts built models of these aircraft and did not use certified values | Expert review |
| 2 | The goals are margins to the new-type level of the carbon dioxide standard of 2017. For the regional jet: the reference of 2017 is 11 % below that level, the goal for 2027 is 16 % below and the goal for 2037 is 26 % below. For the single aisle: 4 %, 14 % and 24 %. For the twin aisle: 4 %, 12 % and 21 %. For the business jet: 13 %, 15 % and 23 %. The values of 2017 are not goals | Expert review, forecast |
| 3 | From 2017 the goals of the regional jet are an improvement of 0.77 % a year to 2027 and of 1.03 % a year to 2037. The highest rate of any class is about 1.3 %. The article concludes that *technology alone will not be able to meet ICAO aspirational goals* of 2 % a year | Expert review, forecast |
| 4 | The goals rest on baskets of technologies taken at nominal confidence, 50 %, and on an optimisation that weighs fuel burn and noise equally. For the single-aisle and twin-aisle classes the baskets give 3 to 4 % less drag by 2027 and 8 to 10 % less by 2037; for a single aisle with a wholly new airframe the experts add 3 % and 7 %. The empty mass falls by 2 to 4 % by 2027 and by 8 to 10 % by 2037. New propulsion saves about 5 to 6 % of fuel by 2027, and an extra 5 % may be possible by 2037. The open rotor was judged unlikely to be in service by 2037 and was not modelled | Expert review, forecast |
| 5 | The experts call the goals challenging but achievable, and write that *a substantial increase in investment in aircraft technology is urgently required*. They note that the fuel burn metric rises sharply from a short range to the design range, so that designing for a shorter range offers major improvements | Expert review |

## What follows for the eWTW

Figures in this section that are not in a statement above are arithmetic of the reviewer, on the figures of the [screening of the cruise points](CRP-EWTW-CRZ-001/README.md).

- **The minimum objective is where the regional jet stood in 2017.** Criterion `M5` asks for a metric 11 % below the new-type level. That is the margin the experts give to their reference of 2017, the E190-E2 (statement 2). An eWTW that meets `M5` and no more is level with a regional jet that is already in service.
- **At nominal values the eWTW of the screening is above the level, not below it.** The screening puts the metric 23 % above the objective at the propeller points and 39 % above it at the jet point. On the scale of the experts that is 9.5 % above the new-type level and 24 % above it. This is by construction: the nominal values of the [mission screening](MSN-EWTW-NRG-001/README.md) reproduce the fuel of the Embraer 190 of the generation before the E2.
- **What each goal asks of the product of efficiency and lift-to-drag ratio.** The screening has 4.02 at nominal values at the propeller points. `M5` needs 4.92. The goal of the experts for 2027 needs 5.21, and their goal for 2037 needs 5.92. The mission screening reaches 5.89 only with every favourable value at once: a lift-to-drag ratio of 18, a turbogenerator at 40 %, an electrical chain at 93 % and a propulsor at 88 %.
- **The favourable values ask more than the experts give.** The baskets of statement 4 take 8 to 10 % off the drag by 2037, and 7 % more with a new airframe: from 15, a lift-to-drag ratio of 16.3 to 17.9. They take about 10 % off the fuel by propulsion. The favourable chain of the screening is 22 % better than its nominal one. That step has still to be shown for a chain of turbogenerator, motor and propulsor.
- **Two forecasts agree on the conventional aircraft the eWTW would meet.** By 2037 the goal of the experts is 17 % less fuel than the reference of 2017, by the ratio of the margins. By the assessment of 2022 a tube-and-wing regional jet uses 88 % of the energy of the reference in 2037, by interpolation, and an advanced concept 10 to 15 % less than that (statements 7 and 8).
- **A shorter design range is a lever the eWTW has.** Regional jets are designed for ranges well beyond the 2,000 km of the requirement mission — the study of NASA read on 2026-11-17 sizes its aircraft of 98 seats for 2,400 nm — and statement 5 says the metric rewards a design for a shorter range. The screening does not show it yet, because it keeps the airframe and the empty mass of the reference type. It is for the mass by option (`KNOT-EWTW-MAS-001`) to show.
- **The mass forecast agrees with the estimate for composite structure.** The experts take 8 to 10 % off the empty mass by 2037 (statement 4). The [record of the structural mass](KNOT-EWTW-MAS-001_structural-mass-and-additive_2026-11-18.md) found about 8 % for composite structure by another route.
- **A question for the authority.** The legal minimum is a floor, and the north star asks for the greenest option. For an aircraft that enters service near 2037, the experts expect conventional technology at 26 % below the level. Whether the eWTW takes that goal as its target, beside the floor of `M5`, is a decision of the authority. *Note of the same day: the authority decided that it does, and beyond ([record](KNOT-EWTW-CRZ-001_objective-and-premises_2026-11-18.md)).*

These are consequences drawn by the reviewer. They are not quotations.
