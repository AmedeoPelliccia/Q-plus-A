## What follows for the eWTW

- **At 100 seats a blended body burns more fuel, in the one sized comparison read.** It is 4.0 % worse on the mission of the reference type of the eWTW (statement 4), and its span of 142 ft is 43.3 m, beyond the stands of the class ([record of the wings](KNOT-EWTW-WNG-001_wings-and-span-limits_2026-11-17.md), statement 4). This agrees with the authority, who places the eWTW far from a blended wing body. Option C of this KNOT is not that aircraft: it is a tube whose section is flattened to carry part of the lift. No named study supports it or refutes it (statement 8), so it stays open without a number.
- **A better lift-to-drag ratio is not less fuel.** The blended body of statement 4 gains 4.5 % in lift-to-drag ratio and loses 4.0 % in fuel, because its empty weight is 15 % higher and its take-off weight 10 % higher. A screening of the sections of this KNOT has to carry the change of empty mass with the change of drag. The mission screening keeps the airframe of its reference type and does neither.
- **The class sits four abreast in a cabin of 2.74 m or less.** The Embraer 190 gives 0.685 m of cabin width for each seat abreast, with an aisle of 0.49 m and a cabin 2.00 m high (statement 1). These are the first measures a floor of comfort can be written in. The value of the floor is the authority's.
- **The cabin of the reference type is smaller than the screening assumes.** The study gives the ERJ190 about 717 ft², 66.6 m², for 98 passengers (statement 5). The [screening of the cruise points](CRP-EWTW-CRZ-001/README.md) assumes 60 to 95 m² with 75 m² as nominal. The figure is not defined, so the assumption stays; it is no longer without a point of comparison.
- **The question on hydrogen is not answered.** The report sets it aside because ruling R1 excludes hydrogen from this model. The brief asks it because the authority states that the wide tube is the ground for an architecture better suited to tanks of liquid hydrogen. Statement 10 names no design, no section and no tank volume. What that purpose asks of the section is still without a value. *Note of 2026-11-18: read since, in two named sources that disagree ([record of 2026-11-18](KNOT-EWTW-FUS-001_wide-sections-and-hydrogen-tanks_2026-11-18.md), statements 10 to 13).*
- **The dimensions of the options are not yet computed.** That is `KNU-EWTW-FUS-001-02`. Statements 1 to 3 give it the reference cabin.

These are consequences drawn by the reviewer. They are not quotations.

## Open

Research can still give:

- the cabin width, the cabin length and the fuselage section of the ATR 72-600, of the Dash 8-400 and of the Airbus A220, read in their airport planning manuals;
- one or more studies of a lifting fuselage near 100 seats, by title, with the section, the lift carried by the fuselage, the wetted area and the lift-to-drag ratio;
- published designs of hydrogen aircraft of 70 to 150 seats, by name, with the section, the place and the volume of the tanks, and the length they add;
- a figure, with its source, for the mass of a pressurised section that is not circular;
- the reference geometric factor of the certified types of the class ([record of the drag data](KNOT-EWTW-AER-001_drag-data-and-forecasts_2026-11-17.md), statement 3).

The rest is work of the programme and decisions of the authority:

- the dimensions each option gives (`KNU-EWTW-FUS-001-02`);
- the measure and the value of the floor of comfort, and the tank the wide tube is to make room for;
- the criteria of this KNOT (`KNU-EWTW-FUS-001-03`).
