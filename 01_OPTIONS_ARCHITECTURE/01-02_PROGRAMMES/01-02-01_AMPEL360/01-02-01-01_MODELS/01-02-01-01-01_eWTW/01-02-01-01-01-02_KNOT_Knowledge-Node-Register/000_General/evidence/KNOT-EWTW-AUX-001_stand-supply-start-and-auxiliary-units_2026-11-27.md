This record keeps what the review accepted from report C and from the assessment of it. It is a reading of public sources, written in our own words. It recommends no option, and it states no conformity of the eWTW with any rule.

**Safety first.** Nothing here ranks an option or sizes a unit, and no criterion of the KNOT changes. Three statements bear on persons before they bear on performance. The maker of the one aircraft with a hotel mode reports four starts made without the propeller brake while a start in hotel mode was intended (statement 17): a hazard to persons at the stand that the record of 2026-11-26 did not hold. An auxiliary unit that starts on the battery of the critical loads may shorten the flight (statement 28). And what no ground supply covers at the stand is, with passengers on board, the air of their cabin (statements 1, 2): no threshold for it is traded. On the eWTW the propellers are electric: a turbogenerator that runs at the stand turns none unless the drives are fed. Report C draws from it that the drives need a positive inhibition; that is a derivation, with no source.

**Who read what.** In the last column of each table, *two readers* means that report C gives the statement from the source named and that the reviewer read the place cited, on its own. Where the reviewer read more than report C gives, the column says which part has one reader. *Reviewer* means the reviewer alone. *As reported* means that only report C read the source. Report C read the record of 2026-11-26 before it wrote: where it repeats a statement of that record with the same source and no detail of its own, the review does not count it as a second reader, and that record gains a second reading on two of its statements only, 7 and 30. The reviewer read through a tool that answers questions on a text and does not return it; every figure is to be read again at the document before it is used. The identifier after each reading is that of the assessment of 2026-11-27.

**Words.** *Preconditioned air* is conditioned air from a unit on the ground, through a hose; the records also say *ground air*. *Code C* and the other codes are the letters by which the airport of statement 1 classes its stands; the code of the eWTW is not on record. The sources say *auxiliary power unit* for a gas turbine that drives no propulsor; the model says *auxiliary power module* at `049` and does not determine its technology. A rating in kVA is the ceiling of a power in kW.

## What follows for the eWTW

Each point is a derivation of the reviewer and ranks nothing.

- **What the class draws at the gate is now a number, and it is the draw of aircraft whose packs are off.** Regional jets at one airport: 7.6 to 12.5 kW at the median above a cut of 7 kW, and ground power for none to 14 % of their time at the gate at the median (statements 4, 5). These aircraft take their air from an auxiliary unit or from the ground. It is no load for the eWTW.

Two schedules of all loads at the gate, for 90 passengers, come to 86 and 107 kW: near or above the one connection of 90 kVA that a stand for a Code C aircraft has at the airport read (statement 1). On energy there are two bounds, both small beside the tens of kWh of a stand with no ground air: a study allocates 0.78 kWh (statement 21); and if the four starts in a row were made without recharging the bank — the abstract does not say — one start took a quarter of 4.03 kWh at most, about 1 kWh nominal.
