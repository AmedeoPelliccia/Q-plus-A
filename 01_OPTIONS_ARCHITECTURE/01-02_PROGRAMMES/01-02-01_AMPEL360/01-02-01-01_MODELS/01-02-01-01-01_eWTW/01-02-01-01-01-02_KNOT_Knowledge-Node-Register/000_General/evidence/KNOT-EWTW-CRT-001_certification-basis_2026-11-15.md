## What follows for the eWTW

- **The basis the records read is the one in force.** CS-25 stands at Amendment 28 (statement 1), and the five paragraphs the brief names are unchanged by it (statement 2). All five are now read at the Agency. The texts of 2026-11-13 that were held as reported are confirmed (statement 6).
- **A turbogenerator used for propulsion falls under the definition of an engine.** Statement 13 answers the question added to the brief of `KNU-EWTW-CRT-001-02`, which the report says has no published answer. A memorandum is guidance and not a rule, and it speaks of the type certificate, not of CS-25. Read with statement 9, it puts the turbogenerators of the eWTW inside the propulsion system and under the definition of an engine. The isolation and the rotor-burst precautions of statement 4 are written for engines.
- **What the other reading would change.** Under Subpart J (statement 8) an essential auxiliary power unit has the same precaution against rotor failure in words, and no isolation between units is required. The difference read between the two is the isolation and the specification the machine meets, CS-E or CS-APU, not the wording on rotor burst. A unit to CS-APU is admitted only under conditions agreed with the Agency (statement 13). The exception from CS 25.1309(b) in statement 8 was not compared with the paragraph on the installation of engines, CS 25.901, which was not read in full.
- **No text says which machines are the engines that the climb requirement counts.** With several turbogenerators and several electric engines, both kinds are engines by statements 9 and 13. CS 25.121 counts two, three or four, and names no machine. The proposed means of statement 10 lets the applicant propose how the loss of one is classified. So the failure case that sizes the generation and the propulsors has no text, and finding 16 of the [record of the generation architecture](KNOT-EWTW-GEN-001_generation-architecture_2026-11-15.md) has its reason: the power to be left after the loss of one unit is a position to seek, not a figure to find.
- **A position the programme can take meanwhile.** The four-engine gradients are the most severe of statement 3. A screening can hold them after the loss of the most critical single unit, of either kind, and say that it is an assumption. That is a proposal of the reviewer for `KNU-EWTW-GEN-001-02`; it is not in any text.
- **No published method demonstrates a turbogenerator.** The one means that is final has a section named for turbo generators, and it is reserved (statement 11). A certified turbine brings the credit of its own type certificate, and the guidance for the rest is to be developed. That is an item of maturity for `KNOT-EWTW-GEN-001` and for both options of `KNOT-EWTW-PRP-001`.
- **A propulsion battery needs a special condition that does not exist.** CS 25.1353(c) does not name lithium (statement 7). The special conditions on record were written for batteries of aircraft systems (statement 14, finding 20). If a propulsion battery is carried, it meets the aircraft requirements wherever its certificate sits (statement 13). That weighs on option C of `KNOT-EWTW-NRG-001` and on `KNOT-EWTW-STO-001`, as a risk of the programme and not as a ruling.
- **Where the type certificates divide is a choice of the programme.** Statement 13 allows the propulsion system inside the aircraft certificate, or an engine certificate that can extend to the power management and to the battery, with the tanks and the fuel distribution left to the aircraft. The PBS groups `060` and `070` will have to say on which side each product stands. Nothing decides it today.
- **The fuel of the eWTW is inside the special condition; hydrogen is not.** Statement 9 covers petroleum-based, synthetic and bio fuels. A model of the family that carries hydrogen is outside this special condition. It bears on the reopen condition of ruling R1 of `KNOT-EWTW-NRG-001`, not on the eWTW as ruled.
- **The final reserve stays the one approved.** The point is written in time for fuel or energy (statement 16). Ruling R2 approved 30 minutes. Whether an aeroplane whose thrust comes from electric engines fed by gas turbines is a turbine-engined aeroplane under that point is not written anywhere (finding 18).

## Open

Research can still give:

- the special conditions EASA has attached to types for rechargeable lithium batteries, read at the source, and the later ones of the FAA;
- the other means of compliance to SC E-19 and their state, one by one: ratings, overspeed and containment, calibration, and the final issue of the teardown inspection;
- any position of EASA or of the FAA on the climb requirement for an aeroplane with more than four engines, in a published issue paper or special condition for a distributed-propulsion type;
- the wording of AMC 25.1309 at Amendment 28, which statement 2 says was amended.

The rest is work of the programme and decisions of the authority:

- the declaration of the basis (`KNU-EWTW-CRT-001-03`), from the table above;
- the positions to seek, each with the nearest text in force (`KNU-EWTW-CRT-001-02`);
- the failure case of the screening, as an assumption until a position exists (`KNU-EWTW-GEN-001-02`);
- the choice between the two approaches of statement 13, and with it the side of each product of the PBS groups `060` and `070`.
