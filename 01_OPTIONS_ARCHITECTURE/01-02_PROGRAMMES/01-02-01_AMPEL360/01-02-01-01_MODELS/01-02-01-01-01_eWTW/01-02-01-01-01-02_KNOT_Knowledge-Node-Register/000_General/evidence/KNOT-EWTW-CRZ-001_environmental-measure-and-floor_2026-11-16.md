This record keeps what the review accepted from the two reports. It is a reading of public sources, written in our own words. It recommends no option, and it states no conformity of the eWTW with any rule.

The north star of the programme is rule 19 of CM-004. For the cruise point its measure is the fuel on the scoring mission, and a floor on the cruise speed keeps it from choosing an aircraft that is not used. The two reports ask what else the measure can count, and what stands behind the floor.

## What follows for the eWTW

### The measure

- **The north star stays fuel, and a published measure of the same kind exists.** The metric of statement 1 is the fuel for each kilometre in cruise, at the altitude and speed the applicant chooses, set against the size of the cabin. It applies to a new propeller-driven type above 8,618 kg. Criterion `T2` measures fuel on a mission; the certified metric measures it in cruise. The programme can compute the metric of the eWTW when a cabin area and a drag polar are on record. Neither is today.
- **The limit is known at the masses of the screening.** The formula of statement 1 gives 0.67 kg/km at a take-off mass of 44.1 t and 0.72 kg/km at 51.8 t. The standard of statement 2 lowers them by 10 % or by 3 %; which of the two applies at these masses was not read. These figures are the reviewer's arithmetic on the formula.
- **A published bar exists for the word ecological.** For an aircraft of this mass, statement 3 asks for a metric 11 % below the new-type limit, 0.60 kg/km at 44.1 t, and from 2028 for certification on 100 % sustainable fuel. It is a criterion for financing manufacture, not for certification, and its first period ends in 2027. Whether the programme takes it as a threshold of the north star is a decision of the authority.
- **The effects other than carbon dioxide point the same way as fuel.** In the two assessments read, a lower cruise reduces the forcing of nitrogen oxides and the coverage of contrails (statements 7 and 8). They study the world fleet at the altitudes of jets. The jet point at 35,000 ft is inside that band; the propeller points are 8,000 to 10,000 ft below it. Nothing found reverses the order of the proposal, and the jet point is last on these effects as it is on fuel.
- **They cannot be scored.** No figure sets them beside the fuel of a mission (finding 15). The one indicator on record is the cruise altitude itself. To add it as a criterion is possible and would be a proxy; it is not proposed here.
- **Sustainable fuel does not change the order.** It acts on the life cycle of the fuel, which is the same at every cruise point (finding 17).
- **The published metric does not reward speed.** It is taken at the conditions of best range. Like the north star, it leaves the usefulness of the aircraft to another requirement.

### The floor

- **The research does not anchor the floor at 360 kt.** Both speeds are in service, 275 kt and 360 kt. The slower type has the larger fleet and the faster one stopped being built (statement 13, as reported). No rule sets a floor (finding 19), and no figure prices the time (finding 18).
- **It does not refute it either.** The stages the sheets quote are of 200 and 300 nm (statement 10). No aircraft of the class carries 100 passengers over 1,100 or 2,000 km on propellers: the range of the Dash 8-400 with full passengers is 1,596 km ([record of the fuel location](KNOT-EWTW-FUL-001_fuel-location_2026-11-15.md), statement 2). The evidence is about shorter stages than those ruling R2 approved.
- **So `M4` is a requirement of the programme and nothing else.** Its ground is not what operators accept. It is that the missions of ruling R2 are flown by jets today, and that the authority does not want the alternative to be slower than the fastest propeller aircraft in service. That is a choice, and the record of the proposal now says so.
- **Without the floor the order still chooses 275 kt.** The figures of the two makers are 69 and 72.4 g of carbon dioxide for each seat-kilometre (statements 10 and 11): the block fuel of statement 10 on 300 nm, at 3.16 kg of carbon dioxide for each kilogram of fuel, the factor in common use. They are 4 % apart, on two aircraft and on conditions that are not the same. That is inside the margin of 5 %, as in the screening, where the propeller points have equal fuel. Installed power then decides, for the slowest point.
- **The one programme of the class that stopped gave a technical reason.** Statement 12 names engine technology, not the market. It is the same dependency that the records of `KNOT-EWTW-GEN-001` and `KNOT-EWTW-PRP-001` show for the eWTW.

These are consequences drawn by the reviewer. They are not quotations.

## Open

Research can still give:

- the certified metric values of the types of the class in the ICAO database of statement 1, which would compare cruise points on a certified measure;
- where the stricter standard of statement 2 draws the line between large and small aeroplanes, and its new limit at 44 to 52 t;
- the effects other than carbon dioxide by flight level for flights of regional length over Europe, in a form that gives a figure for each cruise point;
- a lift-to-drag ratio against Mach number for the class, and the fuel consumption of a turboshaft against altitude (finding 16);
- a published value of block time for a regional aircraft, in the standard inputs that air navigation bodies use for cost-benefit analysis.

The rest is work of the programme and decisions of the authority:

- the metric of statement 1 for each cruise point, once the cabin area and a drag polar of the eWTW are on record;
- whether the margin of statement 3 becomes a threshold of the north star;
- whether the cruise altitude enters the criteria as an indicator of the effects other than carbon dioxide;
- the floor `M4`: to keep it at 360 kt as a requirement of the programme, to move it, or to state it as a time on a mission of ruling R2.

## Decisions that followed

On 2026-11-16, after this record, the authority decided two of these points.

- **The floor stays, stated in knots.** `M4` keeps 360 kt.
- **The most stringent of the carbon dioxide requirements in law is the minimum objective of the aircraft.** Of the limits of statements 1 to 3 that is the margin of the EU taxonomy, 11 % below the new-type limit. It is criterion `M5` of the KNOT, proposed like the others.

The [screening of the cruise points](CRP-EWTW-CRZ-001/README.md), in its third issue, computes the metric at first order with an assumed cabin area. At nominal values no point meets the objective: the propeller points stand 23 % above it and the jet point 39 %.
