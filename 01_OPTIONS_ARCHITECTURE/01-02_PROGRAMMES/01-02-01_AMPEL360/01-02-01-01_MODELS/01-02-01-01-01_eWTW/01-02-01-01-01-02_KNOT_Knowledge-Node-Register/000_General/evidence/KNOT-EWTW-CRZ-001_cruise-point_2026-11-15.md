## What follows for the eWTW

- **The class flies two regimes, and the options are restated on them.** Propellers cruise at 275 to 360 kt and up to 25,000 ft; fans at Mach 0.78 and above 30,000 ft. The two points first written in the options came from a design study of aircraft of 220 and 250 passengers. Options A and B now name the points in service.
- **The take-off band of the mission screening has its two sources.** Statement 1 gives 0.16 kW of take-off power for each kilogram of take-off mass, and statement 2 gives 0.23 at normal and 0.26 at maximum take-off power. The screening assumed 0.16, 0.20 and 0.25. The band is that of two turboprops, and it says nothing of an aircraft that cruises at Mach 0.78.
- **A turboprop of the class cruises close to its take-off power.** The maximum cruise power of statement 2 is 86 % of the normal take-off power of the same engine. If the turbogenerators of the eWTW are sized in the same way, a battery that only shaves the take-off peak removes little of their rating. The screening by cruise point has to compute it.
- **The propulsor follows the cruise Mach.** Propellers lose efficiency toward Mach 0.8 and fans toward lower Mach numbers (statement 5). Nothing measured compares the two at one Mach (finding 14), so `KNOT-EWTW-PRP-001` cannot choose the propulsor before this KNOT is ruled.
- **The rule fixes the cabin; the cruise altitude then sets the compression.** With the cabin at 8,000 ft or below, the pressure ratio that the cabin air compressors work against depends on the cruise altitude alone.
- **The insulation margin falls with altitude by a measured amount.** On the specimens of statement 9 the inception voltage is 83 % of its sea-level value at 500 mbar, 71 % at 250 mbar and 57 % at 100 mbar. These are specimens, not a harness (finding 16).

These are consequences drawn by the reviewer. They are not quotations.

## Not accepted

| Content of the report | Reason |
|---|---|
| That a stage of 500 km flown at 270 kt takes roughly 30 % longer than at 450 kt, from the difference in airspeed alone | At constant speed the time is in the inverse ratio of the speeds, 67 % longer. A block time adds fixed parts and is another figure; the manufacturers' figures of statements 1 and 2 give it for the turboprops |
| A maximum cruise of 270 kt for the ATR 72-600 | The factsheet gives 275 kt |
| The specimens of statement 9 as winding specimens | They are twisted pairs of magnet wire |
| An experimental study of 2015 on a small ducted propeller, and surface-discharge tests on alumina and FR4 | Neither is identified by title or link |
| A performance sheet of the Embraer 190 of June 2014 | Not identified. The Mach of the charts is kept from the airport planning manual read on 2026-11-14 |
| A range of 2,450 nm for the AR version of the Embraer 190 | Not read. The figure read for the LR version is 2,400 nm |

## Open

Research can still give:

- the cruise Mach, altitude and ceiling of the jets of the class, read at a source that opens;
- block time and fuel of a jet of the class on stages of 500 and 1,100 km, to set beside statements 1 and 2;
- measured propeller and fan efficiencies at Mach numbers between 0.6 and 0.8, in the test reports behind statements 5 and 6;
- the power of cabin air compressors against altitude, from the suppliers of electric environmental control systems.

The rest is work of the programme and decisions of the authority:

- the screening of the missions at each candidate point (`KNU-EWTW-CRZ-001-02`);
- the criteria of this KNOT, with weights and thresholds (`KNU-EWTW-CRZ-001-03`);
- the voltage of the propulsion network, which turns the insulation margin into a requirement once it is chosen.
