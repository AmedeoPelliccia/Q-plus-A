## What follows for the eWTW

- **Each of the two options has a precondition that nothing read meets.** The first-order screening puts the take-off power of option B near 8.8 MW on the requirement mission ([`MSN-EWTW-NRG-001`](MSN-EWTW-NRG-001/README.md)). Concentrated on two to four propulsors, that is 2.2 to 4.4 MW each before any margin for a failed one: the one motor of 2 MW read was cancelled before it flew (statement 5), and the motor of 1.46 MW is a stator test (statement 6). Distributed on more than four, the climb requirement gives no gradient (statement 10), and the text that would count many engines as tolerance of a fault is a proposal about the propulsion system, not about the performance of the aeroplane (statement 13).
- **The cruise speed is the input that is missing.** The gap between a propeller and a fan in statement 8 is between two cruise points, Mach 0.62 and Mach 0.78. The missions of the eWTW fix distances, seats and reserves, and no cruise Mach or altitude. Until one is set, a propeller and a ducted fan cannot be compared for this aircraft.
- **Nothing is measured at this class.** The benefit of thrust outboard is a direction on a six-seat aeroplane, with no figure (statement 2). The lift of a blown wing is indicated on a truck by a test its author does not offer as verification (statement 3). The mission screening credits no aero-propulsive benefit, and nothing read gives a figure to credit.
- **The electric chain needs its own heat sink, and no figure exists at this power.** A turbogenerator rejects most of its heat in the exhaust; the motors, the inverters and the feeders do not. What that costs in drag is not measured (statements 6 and 7).
- **A count of propulsors is also a count of failure cases.** The same number that spreads the power decides how many propulsors are lost in the sizing case, and statement 15 reports that the answer moves the take-off mass by up to 17 %.
- **This KNOT has no criteria and no maturity records.** The records approved by ruling R2 belong to the decision of the energy carrier. They cover the motor, the high-voltage distribution and the thermal management, and not the propulsor or a gearbox.

These are consequences drawn by the reviewer. They are not quotations.

## On `KNU-EWTW-PRP-001-05`

The report says that an open option B is not a ruling that admits the thermal machine, and that opening the PBS group now would get ahead of it. On the letter of the condition it is right: this KNOT has no ruling.

What the record holds is this. Ruling R2 of `KNOT-EWTW-NRG-001` is complete and removes the all-electric chain, so option B is the only one left on that axis. The exclusion of hydrogen rests on ruling R1, which is provisional. The author asked for the group on 2026-11-14, and it is in a change that only his merge ratifies.

So the group is opened ahead of the ruling of this KNOT, by an act of the author, on R2. It is a scaffold with one chapter and no station. It is retired if the thermal machine leaves the option set, which can happen if R1 is not completed as it stands or if option A is reopened and chosen.

## Open

Research can still give:

- the text of CS 25.121 and of CS 25.903(b) at the amendment in force, and of EHPS.80 in SC E-19;
- the paper behind statement 15, with its aircraft and its cases;
- measured efficiencies of a complete motor with its inverter at a megawatt, on a bench at full load;
- what propulsor counts and unit ratings the published concepts of 70 to 100 seats adopt.

The rest is work of the programme and decisions of the authority:

- the cruise Mach and altitude of the eWTW;
- the criteria of this KNOT, with weights and thresholds, and its maturity records (`KNU-EWTW-PRP-001-01`);
- the number of propulsors and their rating, with the failure case that sizes them (`KNU-EWTW-PRP-001-02` and `-04`);
- the certification basis for a number of propulsors above four;
- the completion of ruling R1, on which the exclusion of hydrogen rests.
