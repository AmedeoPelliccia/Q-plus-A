This record holds the decision, the corrections, and what was read for them. The figures that answer it are in the [screening of the configurations](CFG-EWTW-AER-001/README.md) of the same day.

## The decision

The authority wrote on 2026-11-18: *«chiaro che l'obiettivo é il 26% e oltre. la stella polare dice proprio questo, ed anzi fare di tutto per migliorare quel limite.»* The objective is 26 % and beyond; the north star says exactly this, and everything is to be done to better that limit.

The decision answers the question the [record of the technology goals](KNOT-EWTW-AER-001_icao-technology-goals_2026-11-18.md) left to the authority. It is recorded as follows.

- **Criterion `M5` of `KNOT-EWTW-CRZ-001` is restated.** The carbon dioxide metric of the aircraft is at least 26 % below the new-type level of ICAO Annex 16, Volume III, and the aircraft is to better that margin where it can. This is the goal of the independent experts of ICAO for a regional jet of 2037.
- **The minimum objective of 2026-11-16 stays as a floor under it.** That decision took the most stringent of the carbon dioxide requirements in law, on that day 11 % below the new-type level: the margin of the EU taxonomy for an aircraft of 5.7 to 60 t. It is not withdrawn. *Corrected on 2026-11-20. This point called it the legal minimum and said that an aircraft cannot be certified above it. That was wrong. The margin is a criterion of the taxonomy for the manufacturing of aircraft ([record of the environmental measure](KNOT-EWTW-CRZ-001_environmental-measure-and-floor_2026-11-16.md), statement 3), and not a limit of the type certificate. The type certificate follows the carbon dioxide standard that applies to the type: the new-type limit, and the stricter one for types applied for after 2031 (statement 2). A review of the brief of 2026-11-20, forwarded by the authority, pointed it out.*
- **The criterion stays `PROPOSED`.** No criterion of this KNOT has been approved by name, and the restatement changes none of that.
- **Ruling R1 is not rewritten.** It fixed the moment at which `M5` is met: the release of the preliminary-design baseline, with the gap at nominal values recorded as a target at the concept baseline. The ruling was made when `M5` stood at 11 %. Whether that moment holds for 26 % is a question for the authority (open points).

## What follows for the eWTW

- **The whole eWTW does not have to be rethought.** Nothing read reopens the energy carrier, the missions or the method. What moves is the objective, and with it the weight of two KNOTs that were treated as settled in practice: the propulsion chain and the polar.
- **The objective is not met at nominal values, and not by the choice of section or wing alone.** The [screening of the configurations](CFG-EWTW-AER-001/README.md) puts the aircraft between 5 % and 19 % above the new-type level at nominal values, between 8 % and 19 % below it with the baskets of the experts for 2037, and at 26 % below in two aircraft of 36 with every favourable value.
- **The electrical chain is where the objective is won or lost.** Statement 3 and the screening agree: with the losses and the mass of a chain of generator, converters and motor, a turboelectric aircraft is behind the same aircraft with a mechanical drive, by about ten points of margin in the screening. Statement 1 reports a gain of 32 % instead. That study changes the airframe and the propulsors together, with the assumptions of statement 2; how it splits its gain was not read. What the propulsors of the eWTW give back has to be shown in `KNOT-EWTW-PRP-001`, and the options of that KNOT that stand — a thermal machine with an electrical chain, distributed or not — include chains in which part of the power does not pass through the electrical machines.
- **The propeller points rest on a statistic.** Statement 6 puts turboprops well below jets in best lift-to-drag ratio at equal geometry, and statement 4 has no figure for them. The screening sets its propeller points on the best ratio of statement 6, which costs them ten points of margin against the drag level of a jet. A measured polar would settle it (finding 9).
- **One check of the scale holds.** Statement 7 and the margin of the experts for the E190-E2 place the Embraer 190 at 7.6 % above the new-type level. The screening, with the metric at the best ratio, places it at 8.5 %.
- **The metric and the fuel are two measures.** The metric is taken where the applicant chooses and does not see the cruise speed; the fuel on the scoring mission does. A larger wing improves the first and costs the second at the floor of speed. Criterion `T2`, the north star, is the one that follows what the aircraft burns in service.

These are consequences drawn by the reviewer. They are not quotations.

## Not accepted

| Claim | Why |
|---|---|
| The saving of 32 % of statement 1 as a figure for the eWTW | It is for 86 passengers and 1,980 nmi, against a jet baseline, with assumptions for 2030 that the study itself tests at half the specific power. Its mass ratio is used in the screening; its saving is not |
| A constant of 11.22 for the eWTW itself | It is a statistic of turboprops in service. The screening uses the best ratio it gives for one type, and shows the result with half the excess of drag and with none |

## Open

- **The moment of `M5`.** Ruling R1 asks for the objective at the release of the preliminary-design baseline. At 26 %, the screening meets it only with favourable values or with levers that are not in the options as written. Whether the moment stands is for the authority.
- **The approval of the criteria.** The ten criteria of `KNOT-EWTW-CRZ-001` are still `PROPOSED`.
- **A measured polar or a cruise fuel flow of a turboprop of the class**, and the certified metrics in the database of EASA: the two figures that would replace the statistic of statement 6.
- **What the propulsors along the span give back**, in figures: the research of `KNOT-EWTW-PRP-001`.
- **A second reader** for the screening of the configurations and for this record.
