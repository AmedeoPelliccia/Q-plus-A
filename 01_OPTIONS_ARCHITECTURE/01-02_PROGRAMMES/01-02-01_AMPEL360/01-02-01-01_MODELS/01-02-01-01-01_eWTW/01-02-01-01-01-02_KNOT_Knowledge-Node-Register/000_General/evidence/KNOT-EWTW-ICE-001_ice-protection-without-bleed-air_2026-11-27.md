This record keeps what the review accepted from the three reports, from the assessment and from its audit. It is a reading of public sources, written in our own words. It recommends no option, and it states no conformity of the eWTW with any rule.

**Safety first.** Nothing here ranks an option or sizes a system. Four points bear on safety before they bear on power. First, with what ice an aeroplane is shown after its ice protection fails is written in the means of compliance: the ice of an unprotected part, or one half of it where the failure is announced and the crew is told to leave icing as soon as possible (statement 55). It is a rule on ice: it gives no time in icing, no power and no endurance. The essential-loads analysis of the register sheds the protection of wing, tail and propulsors when the normal sources are lost; with options A, B and C that protection hangs on the network that was lost, and with option D on a compressor, or on air bled from a turbine that may be the one that failed. Second, holding ice is the ice of a hold of 45 minutes (statement 54). Third, a surface left unprotected is shown harmless with data, whatever its class (statement 47). Fourth, nothing read shows that a boot sheds on a thin leading edge at the speed of the working reference, and nothing read shows that it does not (statements 33 and 34, finding 86).

**Who read what.** In the last column of each table, *two readers* means that the reviewer and the producer of report B each read the place cited, on their own; the second reading is in report B or in its audit. That producer is one reader however many times it reads: where only it has read a source, the statement is *as reported*. *Reviewer* means the reviewer alone; *of an extract*, that only the passage a search returned was read; *in part*, that the document is longer than what was read. Report C names no source and counts as no reader. The reviewer read through a tool that answers questions on a text and does not return it, and that reads neither charts nor scans. Every figure is to be read again at the document before it is used.

**Documents with a notice.** Four documents of EASA used here carry on every page the footer *Proprietary document. Copies are not controlled*: the explanatory note to a decision of 2015, the type-certificate data sheets of the ATR and of the SAAB 2000, and a special condition proposed in 2019. The agency publishes them on its own site, and the reviewer reads the footer as a notice of document control; the audit would exclude them. Whether they are public material for the brief is for the authority (see *Open*). Each statement that rests on one says so, and none rests on one alone where another source was found. The data sheet of the DHC-8 carries no such footer, as the tool reads it.

**Words.** *Anti-icing* keeps ice from forming: *evaporative* where the water is boiled off, *running wet* where it is only kept liquid and may freeze further back as *runback*. *De-icing* lets ice form and sheds it in cycles, and the ice between two cycles stays on the surface. A *parting strip* is a narrow band at the leading edge that is heated all the time, so that the ice behind it sheds in two halves. *Expulsion* stands here for the systems that shed ice by a mechanical impulse of the skin, whatever drives it. *Boots* are the pneumatic de-icers. *Large drops* are the conditions of Appendix O. Speeds are true, equivalent, indicated or calibrated as each source gives them. Where a source says *engine inlet* of a turboprop, the eWTW has the inlets of turbogenerators that drive no propeller.

## What follows for the eWTW

Each point is a derivation of the reviewer and ranks nothing.

- **Safety first: with what ice the aircraft is shown after it loses its protection is written, and the register assumes the loss.** Assumption A6 of `ELA-EWTW-ESS-001` sheds the protection of wing, tail and propulsors when the normal sources are lost. The means of compliance then takes the ice of an unprotected part, or half of it if the failure is announced and the crew leaves icing as soon as possible (statement 55). That is a rule on ice. How long the eWTW may stay in icing with what is left, and on which source, is shown by this KNOT, by `KNOT-EWTW-ESS-001` and by the propulsion together. On two turboprops read, the procedure for an anomaly of a generator, and one event with both alternating-current buses lost, end with leaving icing (statements 41 and 67); their boots take their air from the engines and not from the network that was lost (statements 27 and 30). With options A, B and C the protection is on that network. An eleventh mandatory criterion is proposed from this text.
- **The hold is 45 minutes in the means of compliance** (statement 54): a shape of ice for performance and handling, and a case for the coincidence of packs and ice protection in the load analysis of `KNOT-EWTW-SRC-001`. It is no reserve of electricity. The coupling of the KNOT with `KNOT-EWTW-SRC-001` names holding. The open input `ice-protection` of `CONOPS-EWTW-ECS-001` names climb and descent, and that concept has no phase for a hold. The assessment proposed to close the difference by naming the hold in that input: an error of the reviewer, who had not read the phases of the concept. It is for the owner of the concept.
- **Boots are not excluded by speed on what was read, and they are not shown either.** A type with boots on wing and stabilizer has a ceiling of 31,000 ft and a maximum operating speed at which the air presses 1.6 times as hard as at the cruise point of ruling R2 of `KNOT-EWTW-CRZ-001` (statements 33 and 34); another has boots up to Mach 0.60 (statement 30). The speed of an aircraft is no condition in which its boots were shown to shed. No limit of inflation or of shedding by speed and radius was found (finding 86), and the means of compliance asks a check to the demonstrated dive speed (statement 40). The open questions of option D are a thin leading edge and its air: bled from a turbogenerator at descent idle or after a failure, or from a compressor of which only a patent was read (statement 39). One air flow was read, for 19 seats (statement 26).
- **The cruise altitude of the working reference is at the tip of what Appendix C calls a possible extent, or just above it, and colder than that tip on a standard day** (statement 53). The altitude does not settle whether protection may be off in cruise: the agency proposed in 2019 that safe operation be shown at all altitudes, or a limitation taken. Ruling R2 leaves the altitude to be closed when the turbines are sized.
- **The one transport with no cabin bleed anti-ices its engine inlets with ducted hot air** (statement 3). The inlets of the turbogenerators of the eWTW are protected whatever the option, by the coupling with `KNOT-EWTW-GEN-001`, and what is asked of engines and inlets holds whether or not the aircraft is certified for icing (statement 57). As the model says, a bleedless environmental control is not an aircraft without bleed.
- **The braced wing has a test to start from** (statement 60): ice on the struts cost drag and little lift, the mid span of the wing drove the loss of maximum lift and the outer span the pitch-up. It is a model at low speed and at a low Reynolds number, with no junction, no fold and no propeller ahead of the wing. For `KNOT-EWTW-WNG-001` it is a precedent for the second criterion, that a surface left unprotected is shown harmless with data, and it is not that showing.
- **No aircraft of the class was found that protects wing or tail without engine bleed air, and no power of a wing of the class in service.** The turboprops read have boots, on engine bleed air where the source says (statements 27, 30, 31, 32 and 33); jets of 50 to 100 seats were not surveyed. The table of report B, built on the settings of a tunnel test at 87 kt (statement 10), has means 2 to 5 times lower than the figures of statement 6: the spread is the finding. A European regional design allocates 37 to 49 kW to its airframe and gives no length (statement 9). Expulsion alone is under 1 kW wherever a source gives a figure (statements 17 and 20), and each is an estimate. None is a load of the eWTW: the protected surfaces of the working reference wait for `KNOT-EWTW-WNG-001` and `KNOT-EWTW-PRP-001`.

- **Whether a means to detect large drops belongs in the product structure waits for the path under CS 25.1420** (statement 49): a decision of `KNOT-EWTW-CRT-001`. Until it is taken, no detector of large drops is common to the options.
- **The table that `KNU-EWTW-ICE-001-02` asks is not here.** Report B offers a method for it, with every input stated: it keeps the mean apart from the peak, the airframe apart from propellers and inlets, and electricity apart from bled air.
