## What follows for the eWTW

Figures in this section that are not in a statement above are arithmetic of the reviewer.

- **A value can now be written for the wide tube.** Three widths are read at a manufacturer: 2.74 m for four abreast, 3.28 m for five, 3.63 m for six (statements 1 to 3). The rule of statement 4 gives the least width of each: 2.57 m, 3.07 m and 3.58 m. Two aisles start at seven abreast, 4.57 m by the same rule, and the two studies read find them worse below about 180 seats (statements 8 and 13). At 100 seats the wide tube is a tube of five or six abreast with one aisle.
- **A wider tube is not more comfort unless the width is spent on the passenger.** The cabin of the reference type gives 0.685 m of width for each seat abreast. Five abreast in 3.28 m give 0.656 m, and six abreast in 3.63 m give 0.605 m. Comfort as the authority states it needs a measure for each passenger — the seat, the aisle, the absence of a middle seat — and not the width of the tube.
- **Option C has a named study, at another size.** A wide fuselage that carries a fifth of the lift saves 10.6 % of fuel by calculation, at 180 seats and with two aisles (statement 5). The gain comes from the lift of the fuselage and from the smaller tail, and the paper holds the fuselage weight equal. Nothing is read near 100 seats (finding 15), where two aisles do not pay. The option stays open, and the number is not one for this aircraft.
- **A section of lobes is the form of a wide fuselage that the pressure allows.** A flattened wall bends and is heavy; lobes tied by a web are not (statements 6 and 9). The reference type itself has a section of two arcs. If option C is kept, it is as a section of lobes, and its mass is still without a figure (finding 16).
- **The two sources on hydrogen disagree on the width.** One design team widened a regional fuselage from 2.8 m to 3.5 m and says the storage gains (statement 10). One parametric study widened it from four to five abreast and found two points more energy, for 2.5 m less length (statement 13). The insulation differs, vacuum in one and foam in the other, and both are calculated. The purpose the authority states for the wide tube is supported by a design choice and not by the one study that isolates it. In that study the cost of the width at this size is small.
- **The energy of the requirement mission is 2.4 to 2.8 t of hydrogen.** The mission screening loads 6,839 kg of fuel, and the jet point 7,727 kg, at 43.0 MJ/kg, which the screening holds as an assumption. The block energy and block fuel of statement 10 give 120 MJ/kg for hydrogen. The eWTW lies between the two designs of FlyZero: 1.2 t behind a cabin of 3.5 m, and 3.9 t behind a fuselage of 5 m. This sizes nothing on this model, which carries no hydrogen; it is the first measure of the tank the wide tube would make room for.
- **The cost of the width on this model has a first cause.** The study of statement 13 names the mass of the fuselage, before its drag. The dimensions by option (`KNU-EWTW-FUS-001-02`) therefore go with a mass by option (`KNOT-EWTW-MAS-001`).

These are consequences drawn by the reviewer. They are not quotations.
