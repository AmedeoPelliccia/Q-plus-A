## What follows for the eWTW

- **The fuel of the requirement mission does not fit a turboprop of the class.** The [mission screening](MSN-EWTW-NRG-001/README.md) loads 6,839 kg on the requirement mission at nominal values, and the [screening of the cruise points](CRP-EWTW-CRZ-001/README.md) loads 7,727 kg at the jet point. The two turboprops hold 5,000 and 5,318 kg (statements 1 and 2). At the density of statement 3 the two loads are 8,520 and 9,620 litres.
- **The requirement mission is beyond the largest turboprop of the class.** The Dash 8-400 flies 1,596 km with full passengers (statement 2). The requirement mission is 2,000 km with 100 passengers. That is a fact for `KNOT-EWTW-CRZ-001`: a propeller cruise is in service, but not on this mission.
- **The fuel fits the wing of the reference, and no record gives the eWTW that wing.** The mission screening keeps the airframe of the Embraer 190 as it is. Its wing holds 12,971 kg (statement 3): the two loads above are 53 % and 60 % of it. If the eWTW keeps that wing, option A has the volume with a margin. No record defines the wing of the eWTW. A wing follows the cruise point and the take-off mass, so this KNOT now depends on `KNOT-EWTW-CRZ-001`.
- **A tank in the fuselage pays for three things the rules name.** Within the fuselage contour the factor K doubles, forward from 4.5 to 9 and sideways from 1.5 to 3 (statement 4). The tank is enclosed, fumeproof and fuelproof, against the personnel compartments (statement 7). Its flammability exposure is held to that of a wing tank or to 3 % (statement 8), and a tank in the fuselage does not have the cooling of a wing. Lines and components in the fuselage add statement 9. None of the three has a mass on record (finding 14).
- **The report does not cite the paragraph on flammability.** Statement 8 was read by the reviewer beside the paragraphs the report names. It bears on options B and C more than any of them.
- **The bay was the place of a fuel tank before.** Station `053-900-050` of the product tree is titled *Vacated Auxiliary Fuel Tank Compartment Footprint*. Its note says that the auxiliary fuel tank is deleted and that the footprint is kept inert, with its attach provisions. The note was written when the bay was meant for batteries. It is the first input of `KNU-EWTW-FUL-001-02`: under option A the footprint stays vacated, under options B and C it is the candidate place of a tank. To restate the note is an act of the author of the PBS.
- **The bay has no volume on record.** The records of `053-900` give no dimension. Options B and C cannot be screened before an envelope of the bay exists. Option B alone would put 8.5 to 9.6 m³ of fuel, and its expansion space, inside the fuselage.
- **The centre of gravity cannot be computed yet.** The fuel is about 15 to 17 % of the take-off mass in the two screenings. Its place matters, and no geometry on record gives an arm.
- **The place of the fuel and the position of the turbogenerators are chosen together.** The fragments of a rotor leave near its plane ([record of the generation architecture](KNOT-EWTW-GEN-001_generation-architecture_2026-11-15.md), statement 12), and statement 4 asks that no fuel be released near the engines. The two KNOTs are now coupled in their records.

These are consequences drawn by the reviewer. They are not quotations.

## Not accepted

| Content of the report | Reason |
|---|---|
| Protection against crushing and scraping as a requirement of CS 25.963(e) | Paragraph (e) is on access covers. Crushing and scraping are in the acceptable means to paragraph (d), and the tearing away of an engine mount or a landing gear in (d) itself |
| The factors K as 4.5 or 9 forward, 1.5 aft and 3.0 sideways | Incomplete. Sideways the factor is 1.5 outside the contour, and the paragraph adds 6 downward and 3 upward (statement 4) |
| That venting has to prevent explosive vapour mixtures, with the means left to the applicant | The paragraph on vents read asks for other things (statement 8). Explosion prevention is CS 25.981, which the report does not cite |
| A usable fuel near 5,090 kg for the ATR and a volume near 6,500 litres for the Dash 8, from secondary sources | The figures of the manufacturers are kept, as the report itself prefers |
| Tanks of 6,250 to 6,650 litres for the two turboprops | Arithmetic at an assumed density of 0.80 kg/l. The sheets give masses, and the record keeps masses |
| Hydrogen-tank studies in which the range of the centre of gravity roughly doubles with a tank aft | No study is identified, and the carrier is not the one of the eWTW |
| The 727 and the DC-10 as examples of tanks in the centre section | Not read |

## Open

Research can still give:

- types of 70 to 100 seats with a tank in the centre section or in the fuselage, and what their manufacturers publish of it; the report examined three types, all with wing tanks only;
- auxiliary fuel tanks installed in the cargo holds of airliners, with their capacity and their empty mass, as a measure of what a tank in the fuselage weighs;
- the wing area and the fuel volume of the jets of the class, to relate one to the other;
- CS 25.981 with its appendix on flammability, and AMC 25.963(d), read in full for a tank inside the pressure shell.

The rest is work of the programme and decisions of the authority:

- the wing of the eWTW at each candidate cruise point, and the fuel its box holds (`KNU-EWTW-FUL-001-01`, after `KNOT-EWTW-CRZ-001`);
- an envelope of record for the bay `053-900`, without which options B and C have no volume;
- the content of the bay and the fate of its five stations under each option (`KNU-EWTW-FUL-001-02`), station `-050` first; changing the PBS is an act of its author;
- the travel of the centre of gravity, once the wing and the bay have a geometry;
- the criteria of this KNOT, with weights and thresholds (`KNU-EWTW-FUL-001-03`).
