This record keeps what the review accepted from the four reports, from the assessment and from the reading of report A. It is a reading of public sources, written in our own words. It recommends no option, and it states no conformity of the eWTW with any rule.

**Safety first.** Nothing here ranks an option or sizes a system. Four points bear on persons before they bear on power. First, after a probable failure the fresh air may not stay below 0.18 kg per person and minute for more than five minutes (statement 37); the author takes the loss of one pack in flight as a design case with that floor, and for 104 occupants it is 0.3120 kg/s (statement 38). Second, the electric drives of cooling units in service have brought fire or heat into the aircraft: a motor short of a cabin air compressor of the 787 in a bay that can hold fuel vapour, a surge and overheat of an inlet when one compressor ran alone on a failed sensor, worn brushes that set smoke or fire in the tailcone of business jets, and a grounding fault that overheated the structure of a turboprop near its oxygen line (statements 28 and 29). Third, a refrigerant brings a fluid into the pressurised envelope: its leaks are checked from 1 kg of a gas of Annex II section 1, the HFOs among them, or from 3.4965 kg of R-134a (statement 24), and the rules on system safety, flammable fluids and high-energy rotors are named by every report and read by none. Fourth, at the stand there is no ram air: a fan, ground air or a condenser fan replaces it, and the 787 limits ground operation with one ram fan inoperative to outside air below 49 °C (statement 32).

**Who read what.** In the last column of each table, *two readers* means that the reviewer and the producer of reports B and X each read the place cited, on their own; that producer is one reader however many reports it writes. *Reviewer* means the reviewer alone; *of an extract*, that only a passage a search returned was read; *in part*, that the place was read in part. A statement is *as reported* where the reviewer did not read its source: the account of the report that gives it stands until it is read. Where the author gave a primary reference on 2026-11-28, the last column says so: the author is then a reader of that source. Every reading went through a tool that answers on a text and does not return it, so a number can be mis-stated by that tool; every figure is to be read again at its document before it becomes a figure of record.

**Words.** The *air cycle* cools compressed air by expanding it through the turbine of an *air-cycle machine*, after heat exchangers on ram air. The *vapour cycle* cools by evaporating a refrigerant, which a compressor brings to a condenser. A *pack* is the cooling unit of one side. The *cabin air compressors* of the 787 are the electric compressors that feed its packs. *COP*, the coefficient of performance, is cooling over power, as each source defines it: the system coefficient of one source is not the cycle coefficient of another, and each figure keeps its definition. *Ram drag* is the drag of the air taken in to reject heat, net of what its exit gives back. *Class D* is the label of report B for a figure that rests on a transferred assumption. `P1` is a norm or established engineering, `P2` an assumption or a doctrine of the programme, `P3` a derivation of the reviewer.

## The worksheet of report B

The exit of `KNU-EWTW-ECS-001-02` asks a table of power and ram air by phase for each cycle, read by the load analysis of `KNU-EWTW-SRC-001-01`. Report B's worksheet has that form: 25 cases of the concept of operations by three options, 75 rows, steady bus power and sink air, unknowns left blank, every assumption written. Its script replays it byte for byte, and its rows recompute. Its figures are of class D, as report B says, and the author has admitted it as a candidate calculation of that class (first ruling). Five choices drive its figures:

- **Fresh air carries the whole dry gain at 2 °C.** On the stand that gives 1.809 kg/s, 4.2 times the ventilation floor of 104 occupants, and in cruise 0.724 kg/s, 1.7 times. The packs read deliver colder air, and the 787 cools recirculated air as well: either would lower the fresh flow. At the floor the cruise compressor would draw 35.7 kW, not 59.5 kW. Every option carries this choice alike, so it moves the levels more than the differences.
- **Option A is an open air cycle,** with an approach of 8 K, no recuperation and 85 % of the work of the expander returned to the compressor. On the stand it needs a pressure ratio of 1.97 and draws 109 kW against 55 kW for option B; a real three-wheel machine needs its maps, and the factor of two is a property of the model.
- **The aftercooler of option B stops at the cabin setpoint,** so in cruise option B pays 5.4 kW more than option A for cooling that ram air could give; moved below the setpoint, option B equals option A in cruise.
- **The vapour cycle runs at 38 % of Carnot:** 1.96 on the stand and 2.97 in cruise, inside the spread of statements 13, 14 and 19.
- **The cabin gains are invented:** 40 kW on the stand, with 25 and 55 kW as brackets, and 16 kW in cruise. The 40 kW has an order of magnitude on record in the 46.3 kW of the A320 of 186 occupants (statement 13). On a humid stand the worksheet's flow carries 90.18 kW of latent duty.

The one-pack rows put the whole duty on one unit without claiming that it can carry it, as report B says.
